# Character Archetype: The Firebrand

**Version:** 0.0.1
**Status:** Draft Spec
**Category:** Player Archetype / NPC Template

---

## Overview

The Firebrand is an anarchist archetype specializing in gunpowder, fire, and demolition. They reject both English colonial authority and the structured hierarchies of native society. They are chaos agents—not evil, but dangerous. They believe the only honest response to a corrupt world is to burn it down and see what grows from the ash.

In the 1580s-1590s context, "anarchist" means: no king, no chief, no God but fire.

---

## Historical Grounding

### Who Becomes a Firebrand?

**Disgraced Soldiers**
Veterans of the Anglo-Spanish conflicts who learned too well how to destroy. Men who manned the fire ships at Gravelines (1588), who watched the Spanish Armada burn. They came back different. They dream in flames.

**Ship's Gunners**
The men who maintained powder stores on the crossing. They know the weight of a charge, the length of a fuse, the precise moment between spark and thunder. Some of them deserted. Some were marooned. Some chose to stay when their ships sailed home.

**Religious Radicals**
Puritan extremists who believe the Church of England is as corrupt as Rome. They see the New World as a place to burn away the old—literally. Fire purifies. Fire reveals truth. Fire is the only honest sermon.

**The Betrayed**
Men like those Fernandes stranded. Men who trusted authority and were abandoned. Their faith in hierarchy died on Roanoke's shore. What replaced it burns.

## Character Profile

### Core Philosophy

> *"They told us this land was ours to claim. They told us God blessed our mission. They told us the savages needed civilizing. They told us the ships would return. They lied about everything. The only truth I've found comes from the barrel of a gun and the heart of a fire."*

The Firebrand believes:
- **All authority is violence** — Kings and chiefs both rule through force; at least fire is honest about what it is
- **Property is theft** — Fences, deeds, territory lines—all lies backed by weapons
- **The old world must die** — Both English and native ways are corrupted; something new must emerge from destruction
- **Fire reveals truth** — Burn away the lies and see what remains

### Psychological Profile

- **Charismatic but unstable** — Can inspire followers, but prone to escalation
- **Surprisingly principled** — Has a code, even if it's alien to others
- **Protective of the vulnerable** — Hates bullies, slavers, those who abuse power
- **Self-destructive** — Knows they'll probably die by their own flame; accepts it

## Gameplay Mechanics

### Specialization: Gunpowder & Fire

#### Crafting Tree

**Basic Tier**
| Item | Materials | Effect |
|------|-----------|--------|
| Black Powder | Saltite + Charcoal + Sulfur | Base explosive component |
| Slow Match | Hemp cord + Saltpeter solution | Reliable ignition source, burns 1ft/min |
| Fire Bundle | Dry grass + Pine resin + Cloth | Throwable fire starter, small radius |

**Intermediate Tier**
| Item | Materials | Effect |
|------|-----------|--------|
| Powder Bomb | Black Powder + Clay pot + Slow match | Timed explosive, 3-5 second fuse |
| Fire Arrows | Arrow + Cloth + Pine tar | Ignites on impact, reduced accuracy |
| Smoke Pot | Black Powder + Wet leaves + Clay pot | Area denial, obscures vision |
| Fuse Line | Black powder + Paper/cloth tube | Controlled detonation at distance |

**Advanced Tier**
| Item | Materials | Effect |
|------|-----------|--------|
| Powder Keg | Large barrel + 5x Black Powder + Sealed lid | Major structural damage, large radius |
| Greek Fire Pot | Pine tar + Animal fat + Sulfur + Quickite | Burns on water, difficult to extinguish |
| Signal Rocket | Black Powder + Iron tube + Wooden fins | Visible for miles, can start fires at range |
| Hellburner | Boat + Multiple kegs + Clockwork fuse | Ship/structure destroyer (single use, massive) |

#### Combat Abilities

**Passive: Powder Sense**
Can smell gunpowder at distance. Alerts to nearby firearms, powder stores, or traps. Slight glow effect on powder sources in dark conditions.

**Active: Quick Fuse**
Reduce fuse time on any explosive by 50%. Risk: 10% chance of immediate detonation on critical failure.

**Active: Controlled Burn**
Set a fire that spreads in a chosen direction. Requires preparation (clear firebreak on one side). Fire spreads naturally on other sides.

**Active: Powder Trail**
Lay a line of powder on the ground. Can be ignited remotely with spark or fire arrow. Burns almost instantly. Good for traps or timed distractions.

**Ultimate: Scorched Earth**
Prepare an area (building, camp, section of forest) for total destruction. Takes significant time to set up. Once triggered, area is consumed. Permanent terrain modification.

### Resource Gathering

**Saltpeter**
- Found in caves (bat guano deposits)
- Can be cultivated from manure piles over time
- Trade item from native contacts (they use it medicinally)

**Sulfur**
- Rare in Virginia—mostly from hot springs or trade
- Sometimes found in swampy areas
- Prized and carefully hoarded

**Charcoal**
- Made from controlled burns of hardwood
- Willow charcoal is finest grain, best for powder
- Byproduct of any significant fire

## Karma Interactions

### Nature Morality System

The Firebrand's relationship with the karma system is **complicated**.

**Negative Karma Sources:**
- Forest fires that spread beyond control (-20 to -50 depending on scale)
- Burning sacred sites (-30)
- Killing animals with fire (additional -5 per kill beyond normal hunting karma)
- Destroying native structures (-15)
- Destroying colonial structures (-5, less because the land "cares less" about English claims)

**Neutral Actions:**
- Controlled burns (historical native practice—fire was a tool)
- Burning the already-dead
- Signal fires
- Cooking fires

**Potential Positive Karma:**
- Burning invasive species (English-introduced plants)
- Controlled burns that promote new growth (native technique)
- Destroying symbols of colonial oppression (contested—depends on collateral)
- Fire ceremonies with proper preparation and offering

### The Land's Judgment

Fire is not inherently evil in the game's moral framework. The Powhatan and Croatoan used controlled burns for millennia—to clear underbrush, promote game habitat, signal between villages.

The difference is **intention** and **control**.

A Firebrand who burns with purpose, who respects the fire's power, who makes offerings before destruction—the land may accept this.

A Firebrand who burns carelessly, who lets fire spread wild, who uses flame as a tantrum—the land will respond with fury.

**Unique Karma Event: The Reckoning**
If a Firebrand reaches -75 karma through fire damage, the land responds with a **counter-fire**. Spontaneous combustion of their powder stores. A lightning strike that finds them specifically. Fire that refuses to light when they need it most.

The land giveth fire. The land taketh away.

## Narrative Role

### As Player Character

The Firebrand is for players who want to:
- Solve problems through destruction rather than construction
- Reject the moral binary of "colonist good/native good"
- Play a chaotic neutral/chaotic good character
- Experience high-risk, high-reward gameplay

**Starting Scenario:**
The player begins as a ship's gunner who was left behind when Fernandes sailed. They have basic powder supplies, knowledge of firearms, and a burning resentment of the authority that abandoned them.

**Key Choices:**
1. Align with colonists (use skills to defend settlement)
2. Align with natives (become a weapon against English expansion)
3. True anarchist path (reject both, build own power base)
4. Redemption arc (channel destruction toward specific evils)

### As NPC

**Name:** Thomas Crowley (called "Cinder Tom")

**Background:**
Former soldier who served under Drake. Present at the fire ship attack on the Armada. Volunteered for the Roanoke voyage seeking escape from memories. When Fernandes stranded them, something broke. He speaks to fires now. They answer.

**Location:** The Burned Clearing
A section of forest he deliberately scorched. Lives in the ash. Plants are starting to return—he tends them carefully. He burned the old. Now he watches the new.

**Disposition:**
- Hostile to authority figures (English officers, prominent colonists)
- Neutral to common settlers (sees them as fellow victims)
- Curious about native fire practices (respects their controlled burns)
- Protective of children (sees them as uncorrupted)

**Quest Lines:**

*"The Powder Cache"*
Cinder Tom knows where Fernandes hid emergency powder supplies before sailing. He'll share the location—for a price. He wants the player to burn something specific: the colonial charter, stored in White's abandoned house. Symbolic destruction of English claim to the land.

*"Fire Speaker"*
Tom believes fire has a voice. He's heard it since the Armada burned. He wants to find a native elder who can explain what's happening to him. The player can help him connect with a Croatoan fire-keeper, leading to either understanding or tragedy.

*"The Final Burn"*
Tom has identified something that needs to die: the wreck of the *Lyon*, beached on the northern shore. The ship that brought them here. The ship Fernandes piloted. He wants to watch it burn. But others have made the wreck their home. Does the player help him destroy it anyway?

## Visual Design

### Appearance

**Clothing:**
- Scorched and patched sailor's garb
- Leather apron (protection from sparks)
- Fingers stained black from charcoal
- Missing eyebrows (occupational hazard)
- Burn scars on forearms

**Equipment:**
- Powder horn on leather strap
- Multiple slow match coils
- Small clay pots at belt (ready bombs)
- Flint and steel in waterproof pouch
- No sword—carries a boarding axe (for breaching, not dueling)

**Signature Item:**
A brass-capped bamboo tube—a primitive flamethrower. Short range, terrifying, dangerous to user. He made it himself. He calls it "Sermon."

### Animation Notes

- Fidgets with fire-starting tools when idle
- Unconsciously positions self upwind of any flame
- Watches fires with unsettling intensity
- Flinches at unexpected loud noises (shellshock)

## Dialogue Style

**Speech Patterns:**
- Short sentences. Declarative.
- Uses fire/burning metaphors constantly
- Quotes scripture, then twists it
- Surprisingly eloquent when discussing destruction's philosophy

**Sample Lines:**

*On meeting the player:*
> "You have the look of someone who's watched something burn. House? Ship? Faith? Doesn't matter. We're all ash eventually. Question is: do you choose what burns, or does it choose you?"

*On the colonists:*
> "They built their little houses. Drew their little maps. Wrote their little laws. As if any of it matters. As if the land gives a damn about English property rights. I've seen what fire thinks of fences."

*On the natives:*
> "They understand fire better than us. Burn the brush, the deer come to the new growth. Burn the fields, the soil wakes up. We burn to destroy. They burn to create. I'm trying to learn the difference."

*On Fernandes:*
> "I used to dream about finding him. Making him watch everything burn. But he's just a man. Small. Scared. Greedy. Killing him wouldn't fix anything. You can't burn away what's already hollow."

*On God:*
> "The priests say God is love. I've read the Book. God is fire. Always has been. Burning bush. Pillar of flame. Sodom's rain. They just forgot. I remember."

## Balance Considerations

### Strengths
- High burst damage potential
- Area denial capabilities
- Can destroy structures/obstacles others cannot
- Unique crafting tree
- Intimidation factor

### Weaknesses
- Resources are rare and consumed permanently
- Fire spreads unpredictably—can backfire
- Karma consequences compound quickly
- Loud—stealth is nearly impossible
- Powder is vulnerable to water

### Risk/Reward Loop
The Firebrand is always one bad roll from disaster. Powder can explode. Fires can spread. The tools of destruction don't discriminate. High skill ceiling, high consequence floor.

## Connections to Other Systems

### The Weeping Grove
A Firebrand who enters the Weeping Grove faces a unique challenge: fire won't light there. The perpetual rain suppresses any flame. This could be:
- A sanctuary from their own impulses
- A prison that negates their power
- A puzzle (how do you help someone with fire when fire doesn't work?)

### Virginia Dare
If Virginia represents the synthesis of two worlds, the Firebrand represents their mutual destruction. Potential foil relationship—both respond to the collision of cultures, but with opposite approaches.

### Karma Weather
A Firebrand at extreme negative karma will find the weather system actively hostile: lightning seeks them, drought makes their fires spread further than intended, rain falls specifically to extinguish their flames at crucial moments.
